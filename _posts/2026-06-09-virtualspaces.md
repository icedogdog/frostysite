a few years ago, maybe around 2023, i was on a long flight. I don't remember where I was going, or why.

I had downloaded a few podcast episodes on my phone, just help the time pass. Among them was [an episode of 99 Percent Invisible, about the old online game The Sims Online](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5q71XcBZw2w). More specifically, it was a story documenting the hours leading up to the servers shutting down, and the communities that were affected by that.

I've never played The Sims Online. But even so, sitting on that plane, I was desperately trying to hold back tears and not sob my eyes out. It was impossible to ignore the parallels between this community and the VR communities that are so important to me. 

One of the clips included a recording of someone who ran an in-game internet radio station, tearfully expressing his appreciation for all the years together, a goodbye song playing in the background, just before the world shuts off.

I was hit with a sudden, grief-ridden realization of the temporary nature of all of this - the inherent shakiness of the ground we stand on, the inevitable doom that one day VRChat might face. It affected me deeply.

We've seen a similar story play out this year with Rec Room. Regardless of whether Rec Room is the kind of game you personally would have liked, it's heartbreaking to think about the communities were torn apart due to forces that the players were helpless to do anything about. Some of those people will make the effort to keep in touch, but others might never see each other again. 

The pattern seems to repeat itself with nearly every large-scale, centrally hosted game: eventually, the company decides it's not financially sustainable, and the servers go dark.

---

Because of Furality's theme this year, and especially after watching the fireworks show, a lot of these thoughts and feelings have been bubbling back up for me. I've been thinking again about how VRChat, and the communities I meet inside of it, won't last forever. It might be a pull-the-plug moment like The Sims Online or Rec Room, or the platform might morph itself into something unrecognizable or slowly fizzle out, like the path Horizon Worlds seems to be heading down.

Since I heard that episode, I've watched other platforms repeat this same story that the Sims Online went through. The company decides the game isn't financially feasible anymore, and the platform becomes doomed to go dark. It seems like the inevitable fate of nearly every large-scale centrally hosted game.

I still have moments where I worry if I'm wasting my time by spending so many hours in VRChat. But, regardless of whatever the societal expectation is, regardless of whatever a "normal" social life is supposed to be like - in moments like this, I try to remind myself that the bonds I have with the people I care about are extremely important. Those connections might be *THE* most important thing I have.

Even if nothing lasts forever, I want to remind myself to cherish the moments I still have.
